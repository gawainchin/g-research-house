:::heading
text: Why the move looks macro-real
:::

:::paragraph
text: "The yield move is not just social-media breakout theater. Reuters tied the latest leg higher to sticky inflation and investor concern that yields could push toward 5 percent. Brookings' framework remains useful here: when long yields rise without a clean growth upgrade, term premium and fiscal-risk pricing start to matter more. That is a much worse mix for high-multiple leadership than a benign reflation story would be."
:::

:::heading
text: What the market is actually saying
:::

:::paragraph
text: "The tape supports a duration-first framework, but not a full panic call. In the 2026 study window behind this note, QQQ still outperformed SPY even as the 10Y rose materially. That means vulnerability is visible before capitulation is visible. The market is not screaming 'sell everything.' It is flashing that the next leg higher in yields, if driven by inflation or premium repricing, will make long-duration leadership work much harder to hold up."
:::

:::verdict
label: Bottom line
text: "A higher US 10Y should not be treated as a uniform equity-bearish signal. The cleaner question is what kind of rate rise you are getting. If the next leg higher is coming from sticky inflation or term-premium pressure rather than healthier growth, duration gets hit first."
:::
