:::heading
text: Why this angle exists now
:::

:::paragraph
text: "China's AI buildout is increasingly being shaped by physical policy constraints rather than pure software ambition. In July 2024, the NDRC, MIIT, NEA, and National Data Administration issued the Data Center Green and Low-Carbon Development Action Plan. The signal was blunt: by end-2025, more than 60% of newly added computing capacity should sit in national hub nodes, new large and super-large data centers should generally target PUE below 1.25, hub-node projects should aim below 1.2, and newly built hub-node data centers should use more than 80% green power. That is not a narrative memo. It is an equipment and grid-readiness memo." 
:::

:::paragraph
text: "The same official data set shows why this matters for listed exposure. As of March 2024, China's 10 national data-center clusters already exceeded 1.46 million standard racks, with overall utilization at 62.72%. By June 2024, according to the National Data Administration, the eight national hub nodes had attracted more than RMB 43.5 billion of direct investment and more than RMB 200 billion of induced investment. Once the state effectively tells the market where compute has to go and under what energy constraints it has to operate, the monetizable path shifts toward the vendors that make those constraints solvable." 
:::

:::heading
text: Where the real bottlenecks sit
:::

:::bullets
items:
  - "Grid connection and substations: the hardest issue is not whether China can generate electrons in aggregate. It is whether approved AI clusters can get the right power at the right node, with enough grid support and green-power compliance."
  - "In-facility electrical architecture: AI density pushes data centers away from legacy layouts toward modular UPS, HVDC / direct-current systems, higher-efficiency transformers, and tighter distribution architecture."
  - "Cooling and thermal management: more dense compute means more liquid-cooling, row-cooling, CDU, and thermal-control spend."
  - "Backup resilience: batteries and gensets matter because uptime still matters, but this looks more like a procurement beneficiary than the deepest structural bottleneck."
:::

:::paragraph
text: "The Horinger cluster example makes the bottleneck tangible. A 2024 green-power supply demonstration project tied to the Horinger data-center cluster was built around 360 MW of green generation capacity, including 300 MW wind, 60 MW solar, and 64.8 MW / 259.2 MWh of storage, with total investment of RMB 1.7 billion. The project directly serves four data-center users and supports 15,000P of compute with 233.8 MW of load. That is the actual China AI-infrastructure story: not just chips arriving, but power, storage, and network delivery having to arrive with them." 
:::

:::paragraph
text: "The policy language also points straight at the inside-the-fence capex winners. The NDRC plan explicitly calls for optimizing power-distribution methods, upgrading to high-efficiency transformers, deploying direct-current power-supply technology and power modules, and improving cooling systems according to local conditions. In other words: the state is effectively naming the spend buckets. That is why the electrical-equipment layer can be easier to underwrite than model-platform names. You can see the architecture shift in procurement and product mix before you can cleanly see who will earn the economic rent in China's application layer." 
:::

:::heading
text: Which listed exposures look most direct
:::

:::paragraph
text: "Kehua and Kstar are the most obvious modular-power expressions. Kehua disclosed a No. 4 global modular UPS market-share ranking for 2023 according to Omdia, and it also runs self-built data centers in China, which matters because it understands the operating problem from both sides of the table. Kstar's 2022 annual report summary showed RMB 2.184 billion of data-center business revenue, covering UPS, HVDC power supply, precision cooling, batteries, precision distribution, and integrated micro-data-center solutions. Those are not vague AI-adjacent labels. They are the literal bill of materials for energized compute capacity." 
:::

:::paragraph
text: "Zhongheng Electric is another useful tell on where capex is moving. Its 2024 annual report summary describes HVDC systems, 10kV direct-to-240V/336V integrated power modules, system efficiency above 97.5%, and single-module support for 2.4 MW of IT load while cutting footprint by 50%. Management also described materially higher delivered capacity for these systems and fit with dozens of large intelligent-computing-center projects. That is exactly the kind of disclosure investors should prefer: architecture shift plus shipment evidence." 
:::

:::paragraph
text: "On resilience and backup, Narada and Shoto look cleaner than generic battery names. Narada said ICT Research ranked it No. 1 in China's 2024 data-center lead-acid battery market with 20.2% share, and it framed AI and digital-economy growth as drivers of higher power-capacity and reliability needs. Shoto says it ranked first globally in 2023 among telecom base-station and data-center energy-storage battery providers by shipment volume, with 10.4% market share, and that it served 50% of the top 10 Chinese self-owned data-center companies and 60% of the top 10 third-party Chinese data-center companies. Those are still mixed businesses, but the customer map is real." 
:::

:::heading
text: Why this may be better than platform hype
:::

:::numbered-list
items:
  - "The order book is more visible. Power and thermal gear usually show up through project wins, market-share disclosures, utilization, or data-center segment revenue before platform economics do."
  - "The spend is policy-forced. Once green-power ratios, PUE targets, and hub-node concentration targets are imposed, some part of the capex stack becomes non-optional."
  - "The earnings path is less concept-dependent. A company can still sell modular UPS, liquid cooling, or batteries even if China's application layer monetizes more slowly than the narrative assumes."
  - "The market is less crowded. Everyone wants to own the software winner. Fewer investors do the plumbing well."
:::

:::heading
text: What remains unproven
:::

:::bullets
items:
  - "Durability: some of this capex wave is policy-accelerated. If local incentives cool or AI demand undershoots, the order cycle can flatten." 
  - "Purity: most listed names are not pure-play AI infra. They often blend telecom, industrial power, renewables, or broader data-center exposure." 
  - "Economics split: asset owners, utilities, and grid operators may carry strategic importance without capturing the best equity economics; equipment vendors may monetize faster but also face competition and pricing pressure." 
  - "Narrative leakage: once investors discover the theme, low-quality adjacency names will get pulled in. That is where discipline matters." 
:::

:::verdict
label: Bottom line
text: "The strongest China AI-infrastructure trade is not 'buy any power stock.' It is narrower: own the layer where AI demand forces visible electrical and thermal capex. In practice that means modular UPS / HVDC architecture, selected cooling vendors, and backup-resilience suppliers first; broad grid and utility exposure second; platform hype last. The physical bottleneck is real, the state has already named much of the spend, and backlog is a better friend than vibes."
:::
