:::bullets
items:
  - AI data-center power is broader than silicon carbide. The economic winner is usually the company that owns more of the power tree and can ship at scale, not simply the one with the purest SiC story.
  - Infineon already has management-guided AI power revenue large enough to matter at group level. That is a business line, not a slogan.
  - onsemi has real AI momentum, but it is still early relative to its total revenue base. The stock works if AI plus cyclical recovery drives margin expansion, not because AI already dominates the P&L.
  - Wolfspeed is still a restructuring and manufacturing-execution story first. AI helps the narrative, but the company has not yet disclosed enough AI revenue to make that the core equity thesis.
:::

:::heading
text: The first mistake is treating this as a silicon-carbide basket
:::

:::paragraph
text: "AI infrastructure absolutely increases demand for efficient power conversion, high-voltage switching, thermal management, and denser power architectures. But that does not mean every dollar of AI power spend maps one-for-one into silicon carbide. A large part of the monetization sits in the broader power tree: controllers, gate drivers, modules, power stages, and system-level design wins. That is why breadth matters." 
:::

:::paragraph
text: "Silicon carbide is relevant where voltage, efficiency, and thermal constraints get tight enough for the economics to matter. But the investor question is not 'Is SiC good?' It is 'Who captures enough of the spend, at attractive enough margins, with enough balance-sheet resilience, to turn AI power demand into durable earnings?' That question splits Infineon, onsemi, and Wolfspeed very quickly."
:::

:::heading
text: Infineon is the cleanest quality expression
:::

:::paragraph
text: "Infineon has the strongest combination of scale, breadth, and disclosed monetization. FY2025 revenue was €14.662B, with Power & Sensor Systems at €4.208B. Management is explicitly targeting about €1.5B of AI-related revenue in FY2026 and about €2.5B in FY2027. That means AI power is already on track to be roughly 10% of the current group revenue base and more than one-third of the current Power & Sensor Systems segment. That is material." 
:::

:::paragraph
text: "Just as important, Infineon is funding the growth from a position of strength. FY2025 segment result margin was 17.5%, adjusted free cash flow was €1.803B, and management was confident enough to pull forward roughly €500M of AI-related investment. The business is not pristine — FY2025 reported free cash flow was negative because of acquisition-related cash use and the group carries leverage — but the operating profile is still resilient enough to invest through the cycle rather than finance from weakness."
:::

:::paragraph
text: "The obvious pushback is valuation. With market cap around €88.6B, the stock trades at roughly 6.0x trailing sales. That is not cheap for a diversified power semiconductor company. But at least investors are paying for something visible: real AI revenue, real margin, and real execution capacity. If Gawain wants the highest-quality way to own AI power without taking single-product or balance-sheet risk, this is the answer."
:::

:::heading
text: onsemi is the better U.S.-listed trade, but it still needs the cycle to cooperate
:::

:::paragraph
text: "onsemi is a real AI power name, just not a pure-play one. Full-year 2025 revenue was $5.995B, while AI data-center revenue exceeded $250M. That makes AI only a little over 4% of the current revenue base. Management now says that AI data-center revenue should double again in 2026, which would push the contribution toward roughly 8% on a flat revenue base. That is enough to matter, but it is not enough to carry the whole company by itself." 
:::

:::paragraph
text: "The bull case is that onsemi has already done the heavy lifting on capital discipline. FY2025 free cash flow was $1.4B, or a 24% margin, and management says the major investment cycle is largely behind it. Q1 FY2026 revenue was $1.513B with non-GAAP operating margin of 19.1%, while AI data-center revenue grew more than 30% sequentially and more than doubled year over year. Add a $6B buyback authorization and you have a credible rerating setup if industrial and auto demand stop dragging on the rest of the portfolio."
:::

:::paragraph
text: "The catch is that the market is already treating onsemi like a recovery-plus-AI winner. At about $44.0B market cap, the stock is trading around 7.3x trailing sales — richer than Infineon despite a less proven AI monetization path and a more cyclical earnings base. That does not make the stock uninvestable. It does mean the trade is less 'own the secular winner' and more 'own a well-run cyclical that is adding a real AI leg.' If auto and industrial wobble again, AI alone will not bail out the quarter."
:::

:::heading
text: Wolfspeed is not an AI power compounder — it is a turnaround with an AI option attached
:::

:::paragraph
text: "This is where the basket framing breaks. Wolfspeed has the cleanest silicon-carbide identity and the most torque if things go right, but it also has by far the weakest burden of proof. The company emerged from Chapter 11, adopted fresh-start accounting, and is still reporting deeply negative profitability. Q2 FY2026 revenue was $168.5M, non-GAAP gross margin was -34%, and adjusted EBITDA was -$81.8M. That is not what a clean AI infrastructure beneficiary looks like." 
:::

:::paragraph
text: "Yes, there are genuine positives. AI data-center revenue grew 50% sequentially in Q2 FY2026. The Durham 150mm shutdown finished early. The company demonstrated a single-crystal 300mm SiC wafer. Cash reached $1.3B, helped by roughly $700M of Section 48D tax refunds, and debt was reduced. Those are real operating and financing improvements. But the AI claim is still missing the one number equity investors need most: absolute AI revenue. Management is telling you growth, not scale. That usually means scale is still too small to carry the thesis." 
:::

:::paragraph
text: "Optically, Wolfspeed does not look expensive on sales. The stock trades around 3.4x trailing sales and roughly 4.2x EV/sales. That is exactly why it is tempting. But a low multiple on a business with negative gross margin, negative free cash flow, heavy dilution, and unresolved end-market dependence is not automatically cheap. It is a market saying, correctly, that the equity still sits behind an execution gauntlet."
:::

:::heading
text: What would actually have to go right for Wolfspeed
:::

:::numbered-list
items:
  - Wolfspeed has to prove that Mohawk Valley and the 200mm transition can deliver gross-margin improvement consistently, not just one-quarter operational anecdotes.
  - AI data-center revenue has to become large enough to disclose in dollars and large enough to matter relative to a roughly $700M-plus annual revenue base.
  - The balance sheet has to keep improving without another funding shock or another wave of equity dilution.
  - New high-voltage and data-center wins have to diversify the company away from the weaker EV backdrop rather than simply offset collapse elsewhere.
  - The 300mm technology story has to become economic optionality, not lab-theater optionality.
:::
